Registering and voting

Lesson 2 of 8

Time
about 10 minutes
Articles
38, 81 to 83, 86, 101, 136, 138

Kwa ufupi

Kila Mkenya mtu mzima ana haki ya kujiandikisha na kupiga kura kwa siri (Ibara ya 38). Jiandikishe mara moja tu, kwenye kituo kimoja, ukiwa na kitambulisho halisi au pasipoti halali. Hakikisha maelezo yako kwenye verify.iebc.or.ke. Uchaguzi mkuu ujao ni Jumanne, tarehe 10 Agosti 2027. Siku ya kupiga kura una karatasi sita.

Otieno almost missed it

In 2022, Otieno moved from Kisumu to Nairobi for work. On election day he queued for two hours in Nairobi, reached the front, and was told his name wasn’t there. He was registered in Kisumu. He went home without voting.

Nobody stole his vote. He just never checked. (Otieno is made up. What happened to him happens to real people.) This lesson is about not being Otieno.

Your right to vote

Every adult citizen has the right, without unreasonable restrictions, to be registered as a voter, to vote by secret ballot, and to stand for office (Art 38(3)).

“Without unreasonable restrictions” matters. The rules for registering and voting must be designed to help an eligible citizen vote, and must not deny them the chance (Art 83(3)).

Who can register

You can register if you are (Art 83(1)):

  1. an adult citizen (18 or older);
  2. not declared by a court to be of unsound mind; and
  3. not convicted of an election offence in the last five years.

IEBC’s website adds that you can’t register while you are an undischarged bankrupt, and that you need your original ID card or a valid Kenyan passport (IEBC: how to register).

How to register

From IEBC’s website:

  1. Go to a registration centre during working hours, in person, with your original ID or valid passport.
  2. Fill in the Application for Registration form (Form A).
  3. You get an acknowledgement slip. Keep it. IEBC says you don’t need the slip to vote.

Register in only one place. The Constitution says you may be registered at only one registration centre (Art 83(2)). IEBC’s website warns that registering more than once is an offence, with a fine of up to KES 100,000, up to one year in jail, or both.

When can you register? The law requires continuous voter registration (Art 82(1)(c), 88(4)(a)), but IEBC also runs special drives with set dates. For the dates before the 2027 election, check iebc.or.ke. We don’t print dates we can’t confirm.

Moved since the last election? Transfer.

IEBC’s website: “A person may transfer as a voter to another registration centre of his or her choice within the registration period.” So if you moved like Otieno did, transfer your registration to where you live now, before the registration period closes. Ask at your nearest IEBC constituency office.

Check your details: do it now, and again before election day

Go to verify.iebc.or.ke. Type your ID or passport number and your year of birth. It shows where you are registered.

Check three things: your name is spelled right, the polling station is the one you expect, and your ID number is correct. If anything is wrong, go to your IEBC constituency office.

Election day: 10 August 2027

The Constitution fixes the general election on the second Tuesday in August, every fifth year (Art 101(1)). The countdown on IEBC’s website says “10th August 2027 General Election”.

You get six ballot papers, one for each office:

Ballot Office Article
1 President (with a Deputy President on the same ticket) 136, 148
2 Governor of your county (with a deputy) 180
3 Senator for your county 98(1)(a)
4 County Woman Member of the National Assembly 97(1)(b)
5 MP for your constituency 97(1)(a)
6 MCA for your ward 177(1)(a)

Lesson 6 explains what each one does.

What the law promises on the day

  • Your vote is secret (Art 38(3)(b), 81(e)(i)). Nobody may make you show or tell who you voted for.
  • Voting must be simple, and take into account people with disabilities and others with special needs (Art 82(2)).
  • Your employer must give you reasonable time to vote, and can’t cut your pay for it (Election Offences Act, 2016, section 18).
  • The votes are counted at your polling station and the result is announced there, by the presiding officer, before anything is sent anywhere (Art 86(b)). Then results are openly collated and announced by the returning officer (Art 86(c)).

How a president wins

To win in the first round, a presidential candidate needs both (Art 138(4)):

  1. more than half of all votes cast in the whole country, and
  2. at least 25% of the votes in more than half of the counties (at least 24 of the 47).

If nobody manages both, there’s a fresh election within 30 days between the top two (Art 138(5)).

The other five seats are simpler: whoever gets the most votes wins.

What to bring

Carry the original ID or passport you registered with. For anything else about the day (opening hours, what happens if your details don’t show), check iebc.or.ke before the election.

What you can do

  • Check your registration today at verify.iebc.or.ke.
  • Moved? Transfer your registration during the registration period.
  • Not registered yet? Take your original ID to an IEBC registration centre.
  • Help one person who isn’t registered: an older relative, a first-time voter, a neighbour with a disability.
  • Know your polling station well before 10 August 2027.
  • Stay in the queue. If you’re in line and something goes wrong, ask for the presiding officer, calmly.

Quick quiz

  1. What three things does Article 83 require to register?
  2. You registered in Nakuru, but now live in Eldoret. What should you do?
  3. Where can you check your registration online?
  4. How many ballot papers will you get, and which is the smallest seat?
  5. What two things does a presidential candidate need to win in the first round?
Answers
  1. Be an adult citizen, not declared of unsound mind, and not convicted of an election offence in the last five years.
  2. Transfer your registration to a centre in Eldoret during the registration period.
  3. verify.iebc.or.ke.
  4. Six. The smallest seat is your ward’s MCA.
  5. More than half of all votes, and at least 25% in more than half of the counties (Art 138(4)).
