## The 24 hours

Remember Otieno on the dark street in Lesson 1? This time the officer doesn't take a bribe. He arrests him.

What happens next is not up to the officer's mood. The Constitution has a **checklist**, and every item on it is
Otieno's.

## If you are arrested: your checklist (Art 49)

You have the right:

1. **To be told promptly, in a language you understand,** why you're being arrested, that you have the right to
   remain silent, and what happens if you don't stay silent (49(1)(a)).
2. **To remain silent** (49(1)(b)).
3. **To communicate with a lawyer** and other people whose help you need, for example family (49(1)(c)).
4. **Not to be forced to confess** or admit anything that could be used against you (49(1)(d)).
5. **To be kept apart from people already serving a sentence** (49(1)(e)).
6. **To be brought before a court as soon as reasonably possible, and not later than 24 hours** after arrest. If the
   24 hours end at night, on a weekend or a public holiday, then by the end of the **next court day** (49(1)(f)).
7. **At your first court appearance, to be charged, told why you're still being held, or released** (49(1)(g)).
8. **To be released on bond or bail, on reasonable conditions,** unless there are compelling reasons not to (49(1)(h)).

And: you **can't be held in custody** for an offence punishable **only by a fine**, or by **six months in prison or
less** (49(2)).

**Never, under any circumstances:** torture, beating, or cruel and degrading treatment (Art 29). Freedom from torture
is one of the four rights that **can never be limited**, not even in an emergency (Art 25).

**Your phone and home:** nobody may search you, your home or your property, or read your communications, without a
lawful reason (Art 31).

**Children** may be detained **only as a last resort**, for the **shortest time**, and **separately from adults**
(Art 53(1)(f)).

## If you're charged: a fair trial (Art 50)

You are **presumed innocent until proven guilty** (50(2)(a)). You have the right to be told the charge in enough
detail to answer it, to have time to prepare, to a lawyer of your choice, to a **lawyer at State expense if
"substantial injustice" would otherwise result**, to remain silent at the trial, to see the prosecution's evidence in
advance, to a free interpreter, and to appeal (50(2)).

Evidence obtained by violating your rights **must be excluded** if using it would make the trial unfair (50(4)).

## If you're held and nobody knows where: habeas corpus

A person detained keeps all their rights, except those that clearly can't work in detention (Art 51(1)). **Anyone
held has the right to an order of habeas corpus** (Art 51(2)). That is a High Court order to the people holding
someone: **bring them before the court.** Family, friends or an organisation can apply on the person's behalf
(Art 22(2)). This right **can never be limited** (Art 25(d)).

## A case that shows why this matters

In April 2015, a boda boda rider, Josephat Mwenda, was shot by a police officer and complained to IPOA. Human rights
lawyer **Willie Kimani** took up his case. On 23 June 2016, Kimani, Mwenda and their taxi driver, Joseph Muiruri,
were abducted after leaving the Mavoko law courts. Their bodies were found in a river a week later.

On **22 July 2022** the High Court convicted three police officers and a police informer of their murder. They were
sentenced on 3 February 2023 (reported by Al Jazeera and Citizen Digital). One officer was acquitted.

The men accused of killing them got everything the victims were denied: lawyers, time, a judge, and the presumption
of innocence. That isn't a weakness of the Constitution. It is the Constitution. The rules are for everyone, and
that's exactly why they protect you.

## If any of your rights is violated

You have three main routes. You can use more than one.

### Route 1: complain to a commission (free)

| Who violated your right | Complain to |
|---|---|
| A **police officer**: beating, shooting, unlawful arrest, a bribe, a death in custody | **IPOA**: toll free **1559** |
| **Anyone**: any right in the Bill of Rights | **KNCHR**: SMS **22359**, complaint@knchr.org. Every person has the right to complain to it (Art 59(3)) |
| A **government office**: delay, abuse of power, unfair treatment | **Ombudsman (CAJ)**: toll free **0800 221 349** |
| **Discrimination** (sex, disability, age and other grounds) | **NGEC**: toll free **0800 720 187** |
| A **public officer asking for a bribe** | **EACC**: toll free **1551** |

### Route 2: go to court

- **Every person** can go to court claiming a right has been **denied, violated, infringed or threatened** (Art 22(1)).
- You can do it **for yourself, for someone who can't act for themselves, for a group, or in the public interest**
  (Art 22(2)).
- **No fee may be charged to start** the case, formalities must be kept to a minimum, and the court may accept
  **informal documents**, even a letter (Art 22(3)).
- The **High Court** hears these cases (Art 23(1)). It can make a **declaration** of your rights, an **injunction**
  (an order to stop), a **conservatory order** (keeping things as they are while the case runs), **compensation**, a
  **declaration that a law is invalid**, and **judicial review** (Art 23(3)).

### Route 3: get free legal help

The State must ensure **access to justice for all**, and any fee must be reasonable (Art 48). If you can't afford a
lawyer, contact the **National Legal Aid Service**: toll free **0800 720 640**.

## What you can do

**If you are arrested:**

- **Stay calm. Don't resist or run.** Resisting can be used against you and can get you hurt.
- **Ask, politely: "Why am I being arrested?"** (Art 49(1)(a)).
- **Say: "I want to call my family / a lawyer."** (Art 49(1)(c)).
- **You may stay silent.** Don't sign or agree to anything you don't understand (Art 49(1)(b), (d)).
- **Remember names, badge numbers, the station, and the time.**
- **Count the hours.** You must see a court within 24 hours, or by the end of the next court day (Art 49(1)(f)).
- **Ask for bail or bond** at your first court appearance (Art 49(1)(h)).

**If someone you know is arrested:**

- **Find out which station.** Go there. Ask for the Occurrence Book (OB) entry.
- **If you can't find them, or they're held past 24 hours,** call a lawyer or NLAS about a **habeas corpus**
  application, and report to **IPOA (1559)** and **KNCHR (SMS 22359)**.

**If your rights are violated:**

- **Write it down the same day:** what happened, when, where, who, and witnesses. Keep medical records and photos.
- **Complain to the right commission** (table above). Keep the reference number.
- **Go to court** if needed. It's your right, and starting the case is free.

## Quick quiz

1. You're arrested at 4 p.m. on a Friday. By when must you see a court?
2. Can the police hold you in a cell for an offence punishable only by a fine?
3. Your brother was arrested and you can't find him. What court order can you ask for?
4. Does it cost money to start a case in the High Court that your rights were violated?
5. Which number do you call to complain about a police officer?

1. The 24 hours end on Saturday afternoon, outside court days, so by the end of the next court day: normally Monday
   (Art 49(1)(f)(ii)).
2. No (Art 49(2)).
3. Habeas corpus: an order to bring him before the court (Art 51(2), 25(d)).
4. No. No fee may be charged to start the case (Art 22(3)(c)).
5. IPOA, toll free 1559.
