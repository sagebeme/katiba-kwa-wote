## Two candidates for MCA

Otieno's ward has two candidates for MCA (both made up).

- **Candidate A** arrives in a convoy, gives out T-shirts and KES 200 notes, and promises "development".
- **Candidate B** has no convoy. She held two meetings about the ward's budget last year and can name the three
  projects in the county budget for the ward, and what each one costs.

Who'll be the better MCA? The Constitution doesn't tell you who to vote for. But it does give you a measuring stick.
It's called **Chapter Six: Leadership and Integrity**.

## The big idea: serve, don't rule

Article 73 says the power given to a leader is **a public trust**. It must be used in a way that **shows respect for
the people**, **brings honour to the nation and dignity to the office**, and **promotes public confidence** in it.

And the line worth memorising: it gives the leader **"the responsibility to serve the people, rather than the power
to rule them"** (Art 73(1)(b)).

The **guiding principles** of leadership are (Art 73(2)):

1. **Chosen for integrity, competence and suitability**, or elected in free and fair elections.
2. **Fair decisions**, not influenced by **nepotism, favouritism**, other improper motives or **corruption**.
3. **Selfless service** in the public interest: **honesty**, and **declaring any personal interest** that could
   conflict with public duties.
4. **Accountability to the public** for decisions and actions.
5. **Discipline and commitment** in serving the people.

These sit on top of the **national values** that bind every leader and public officer: among them the rule of law,
democracy and participation of the people, human dignity, equality, **good governance, integrity, transparency and
accountability** (Art 10).

## The rules every State officer must follow

Everyone on your ballot is a **State officer**: the President, MPs, Senators, the Woman Member, Governors and MCAs
(Art 260, "State office"). Chapter Six says:

| Rule | Article |
|---|---|
| Avoid any **conflict between personal interests and public duties**, in public and in private life | 75(1) |
| Don't put a personal interest above a public one, and don't **demean the office** | 75(1) |
| A **gift** given on a public or official occasion belongs to **the Republic**, not the officer, unless an Act says otherwise | 76(1) |
| No **bank account outside Kenya**, except as an Act allows | 76(2)(a) |
| No **personal loan or benefit** that compromises integrity | 76(2)(b) |
| A full-time State officer can't take **other paid work** | 77(1) |
| Only a **citizen** can hold a State office; a State officer can't hold **dual citizenship** (with some exceptions) | 78 |

**The punishment that bites:** a person **dismissed or removed** from office for breaking these rules **can't hold
any other State office** (Art 75(3)).

**Who enforces it?** The **Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC)**, set up under Art 79.

## Who can't stand at all

The Constitution bars some people from being elected. For **MPs, Senators and Woman Members** (Art 99(2)), a person
can't stand if they:

- are a **State officer or public officer** (other than a sitting MP);
- were an **IEBC member** in the last five years;
- haven't been a **citizen for the last ten years**;
- are a **member of a county assembly**;
- are of **unsound mind**, or an **undischarged bankrupt**;
- are serving a **prison sentence of at least six months**; or
- have been **found, under a law, to have misused or abused a State or public office, or broken Chapter Six**.

**MCAs** have nearly the same list (Art 193(2)). A **Governor** must be eligible to be an MCA (Art 180(2)). A
**presidential candidate** must also be a **citizen by birth**, must not owe allegiance to a foreign state, and needs
the nominations of **at least 2,000 voters in each of a majority of the counties** (Art 137).

One important fairness rule: a person is **not disqualified until all appeals are finished** (Art 99(3), 193(3)). An
accusation is not a conviction.

To stand as an **independent** (without a party), a candidate needs the support of at least **1,000 registered voters**
in the constituency for MP, **2,000** in the county for Senator (Art 99(1)(c)), and **500** in the ward for MCA
(Art 193(1)(c)).

## How to judge a candidate: a checklist

Use these at a baraza, on a radio call-in, or when someone comes to your door.

**About integrity (Chapter Six)**

- Has any court or official body found you misused public money or broke Chapter Six?
- What businesses do you or your family own? Do any of them do business with the government?
- Will you publish how you voted, and what you did with any fund you control?

**About the job (see Lesson 6)**

- *MCA:* "What's in the county budget for this ward? Will you hold a meeting on it before you vote?"
- *Governor:* "How will you pay suppliers the county already owes? Will you publish every county tender?"
- *Senator:* "What did you do about the last Auditor-General's report on our county?"
- *MP and Woman Member:* "How did you vote on the last Finance Bill, and why?"
- *President:* "How much will you borrow, and what for? Will counties get their full share on time?"

**About how they campaign**

- Are they handing out money? (That's a crime: Lesson 3.)
- Do they speak about other communities with respect?
- Do they answer questions, or only make speeches?

Back to Otieno's ward. Candidate B can tell him what the job is and show what she did. Candidate A is offering a
loan disguised as a gift (Lesson 3). The Constitution's measuring stick points one way. The ballot is still Otieno's.

## What you can do

- **Look up a candidate's record.** Court cases are public on [kenyalaw.org](https://kenyalaw.org). MPs' speeches and
  votes are recorded in Parliament's Hansard at [parliament.go.ke](http://www.parliament.go.ke).
- **Ask the checklist questions** in public, so others hear the answers.
- **Judge the record, not the gifts.**
- **Report**, don't just complain, when a leader uses office for private gain.

## Quick quiz

1. Complete the quote from Art 73: the leader has "the responsibility to serve the people, rather than..."
2. An MCA receives a cow as a gift at an official ward function. Whose cow is it?
3. A governor is dismissed from office for breaking the conflict-of-interest rule in Article 75. Can they run for
   Senator?
4. A candidate was charged with corruption but the case is still in court. Is he disqualified?
5. How many supporters does an independent candidate for MCA need?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. "...the power to rule them" (Art 73(1)(b)).
2. The Republic's, unless an Act exempts it (Art 76(1)).
3. No. A person removed for breaking Article 75 (or 76, 77 or 78(2)) can't hold any other State office, and a
   Senator is a State officer (Art 75(3), 260).
4. No. A person isn't disqualified until all appeals or reviews are finished (Art 99(3), 193(3)).
5. At least 500 registered voters in the ward (Art 193(1)(c)(ii)).

</details>
