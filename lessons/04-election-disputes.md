## 1 September 2017: "null and void"

On 8 August 2017 Kenyans voted. The electoral commission declared Uhuru Kenyatta president, with roughly 54% to Raila
Odinga's 45%. Odinga went to the Supreme Court.

His case wasn't mainly "we got more votes". It was "**you didn't follow the process**": results were sent without the
polling-station forms that prove them.

On **1 September 2017**, the Supreme Court agreed, **4 judges to 2**. It declared the presidential election invalid and
ordered a fresh one. It was one of the very few times a court anywhere had cancelled a presidential election.

The story didn't end neatly. Odinga boycotted the fresh election on **26 October 2017**. Kenyatta won it with about
98% of the votes, on a turnout under 40%. On **20 November 2017** the Supreme Court upheld that result.

The lesson isn't who was right. It's this: **in Kenya, a disputed election goes to court, and the court can say no.**

> Ten years earlier, after the December 2007 election, there was no trusted way to settle the dispute. It ended in
> violence, and more than 250,000 people were driven from their homes (IRIN, January 2008). The courts are the
> alternative the 2010 Constitution built.

## The rules start at your polling station

The Constitution says IEBC must make sure that (Art 86):

- the voting system is **simple, accurate, verifiable, secure, accountable and transparent**;
- the votes are **counted, tallied and announced at each polling station** by the presiding officer;
- results from polling stations are **openly and accurately collated** and announced by the returning officer;
- election materials are kept safe.

Why it matters to you: if the result announced at your polling station isn't the one that arrives at the tallying
centre, that's evidence. Agents, observers and ordinary voters who note the announced figures help keep the count
honest.

## Who decides which dispute, and how fast

| Dispute about... | Goes to | File within | Decided within |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The President's election** | **Supreme Court** (Art 140) | **7 days** after the result is declared | **14 days** after filing. The decision is **final**. |
| **An MP, County Woman Member or Senator** | **High Court** (Art 105) | **28 days** after IEBC declares the result (Art 87(2)) | **6 months** (Art 105(2)) |
| **A Governor** | **High Court** in or nearest the county (Elections Act, s.75(1)) | 28 days (Art 87(2)) | 6 months (s.75(2)) |
| **An MCA** | A **Resident Magistrate's Court** chosen by the Chief Justice (s.75(1A)) | 28 days (Art 87(2)) | 6 months; appeal to the High Court on points of law within 30 days (s.75(4)) |
| **Party nominations and disputes before the results** | **IEBC** settles nomination disputes (Art 88(4)(e)) | (check iebc.or.ke) | |

A petition must be **served** (delivered to the other side) **within 15 days** of filing (Elections Act, s.76(1)(a)).
Service can be direct, or by an advert in a newspaper with national circulation (Art 87(3)).

If the Supreme Court finds a presidential election invalid, a **fresh election must be held within 60 days**
(Art 140(3)). That's what happened in 2017.

## What a court can do

For a county seat, the court can declare whether the winner was validly elected, declare who was validly elected
instead, or order a fresh election (Elections Act, s.75(3)).

## What an election petition is not

- **It's not a protest.** You have the right to demonstrate **peacefully and unarmed** (Art 37). But burning, beating
  and blocking are crimes, and they hurt your neighbours, not the politicians.
- **It's not a WhatsApp campaign.** A court decides on evidence: forms, figures, witnesses.
- **It's not free or simple.** It's a formal court case with strict deadlines. Get a lawyer early.

## What you can do

- **Note the result announced at your polling station** (it's announced publicly, Art 86(b)). A photo of the posted
  result, if allowed, is useful evidence.
- **Volunteer** as a party agent or with an accredited observer group, if you want to watch the count.
- **Don't spread "results" from unofficial sources.** Wait for IEBC's declaration, and for the court if there's a
  dispute.
- **If you have evidence** of a problem, give it to IEBC, to a candidate's lawyers, or to an observer group, **quickly**.
  The deadlines are short: 7 days for the presidency, 28 days for the rest.
- **Keep the peace.** If violence is being planned in your area, report it (see below).

3. Your MCA's election looks rigged. Which court, and what's the deadline to file?
4. Where must votes first be counted and announced?
5. What happened on 1 September 2017?
